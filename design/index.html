One design, every subject

Everything below is what a week of any subject is built from. Approve this once and Quantitative Methods, Accounting, Marketing and the rest are the same document with different words in it.

Ground and ink are Palette B from the brand kit, the Anthropic-style set. Terracotta is the only accent. Tan and kraft are panels and rules, never text. The serif carries the reading, the sans carries anything you operate.

Two accents, and they do not overlap. Terracotta is for anything you act on: the button you press, the question you are on, the thing that is live. Forest is for structure: the bar, the mark, a definition heading, a resource you can go and learn from. If a colour is telling you what to do, it is clay; if it is telling you where you are, it is green.

Three rules that are not negotiable. Text on slate is paper, and text on clay is paper. Never clay text on kraft or manilla: there is not enough contrast between them. Never forest and clay in the same sentence, because then neither of them means anything.

Nothing is downloaded and nothing is embedded. Georgia ships with macOS and with Windows and was drawn for screens, so it is the same face for everyone. The sans is whatever the reader's own system uses for its interface: San Francisco on a Mac, Segoe UI on Windows. That is a face people already read all day, it weighs nothing, and there is no file that can fail to load, on a slow connection or none at all.

Composition of functions

Learn it properly Watch: Introduction to composing functions Practise: Evaluate composite functions

Present where a subject has good outside material. Absent everywhere else, and nothing looks broken when it is.

Composition
Do f first, then feed its output straight into g. The circle is read right to left.
  1. Work from the inside out. Compute the inner value, then put it into the outer function.
  2. Substitute the whole inner output. Every x in the outer function becomes the entire inner expression, brackets included.
  3. Order matters. f ∘ g is almost never g ∘ f. Check with one number if you doubt it.
Worked: f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x² − 3, find (g ∘ f)(4)
  1. f(4) = 9
    The inner function first.
  2. g(9) = 81 − 3
    Its output goes into the outer one.
  3. 78
    The middle value was real, but the question was never about it.
  • Watch out
  • f ∘ g is not g ∘ f. Test it with a single number before assuming.
  • The inner output has to be something the outer function accepts.

On paper

What changes

The bar goes. Each lesson starts a page. The interactive practice is replaced by six questions laid out in full, and an answer key closes the document.

The screen version samples eight questions and marks them as you go. That is right on screen and useless on paper, so the two are built differently on purpose.

What does not

The palette, the type, the measure, the spacing. A printed week and the page it came from are recognisably the same document, which is the whole point of fixing this once.

A4, 18mm margins, the mark and the disclaimer in the footer of the last page.
